## The principle

In a sentence like *Tom sait que tu **es** là*, the verb after **que** is in a **subordinate clause**. Its tense depends on two things:

1. the tense of the **main verb** (present / future, or past);
2. whether the subordinate action happens **before**, **at the same time as**, or **after** the main one.

English mostly works the same way (*he says he is* → *he said he was*), so most of the two tables below will look familiar. The differences from English are what to watch.

## Main verb in the present or future

The subordinate takes the tense that fits real time, as if nothing were reported:

| Subordinate action | Tense | Example |
| ------------------ | ----- | ------- |
| before | passé composé / imparfait | Je sais que tu es venu. / que tu venais souvent. |
| same time | présent | Je sais que tu es là. |
| after | futur simple (or futur proche) | Je sais que tu viendras. |
| after, but before another future point | futur antérieur | Je sais que tu auras fini avant midi. |

- Tom saura que tu as regardé la télé hier. | Tom will know you watched TV yesterday.
- Je pense qu’il pleuvra demain. | I think it will rain tomorrow.
- Elle dit qu’elle travaillait ici avant. | She says she used to work here.

## Main verb in the past

Everything shifts one step back:

| Subordinate action | Tense | Example |
| ------------------ | ----- | ------- |
| before | plus-que-parfait | Je savais que tu étais venu. |
| same time | imparfait | Je savais que tu étais là. |
| after (future in the past) | conditionnel présent | Je savais que tu viendrais. |
| after, completed before a later point | conditionnel passé | Je savais que tu aurais fini avant midi. |

- Tom ne savait pas que tu étais en France. | Tom didn’t know you were in France.
- Il a dit qu’il avait perdu ses clés. | He said he had lost his keys.
- Je croyais qu’il viendrait. | I thought he would come.
- Elle m’a promis qu’elle aurait terminé lundi. | She promised me she would have finished by Monday.

> [!WARNING]
> After a past main verb, the **future** becomes the **conditional**: ~~Il a dit qu’il viendra~~ → *Il a dit qu’il **viendrait***. This “future in the past” is the most common concordance mistake.

## When the present stays: general truths

A fact that is **always true**, or still true now, can stay in the present after a past main verb:

- Mes parents disaient toujours que l’argent ne fait pas le bonheur. | My parents always said money can’t buy happiness.
- Galilée a affirmé que la Terre tourne autour du Soleil. | Galileo stated that the Earth goes round the Sun.
- Il m’a dit qu’il habite à Lyon. | He told me he lives in Lyon. (he still does)

In speech, concordance is often relaxed this way. In formal writing, apply the tables unless the fact is a timeless truth.

## Time clauses: the future after quand

When the time is future, French uses a **future after quand, lorsque, dès que, aussitôt que, tant que, une fois que**, where English uses a present:

- Je serai footballeur quand je serai grand. | I’ll be a footballer when I grow up.
- Quand j’aurai fini, je partirai. | When I’ve finished, I’ll leave.

In a past story, the same clauses take the imparfait (habit) or the plus-que-parfait (completed first):

- Quand il faisait beau, on allait à la plage. | When the weather was nice, we went to the beach.
- Dès qu’il avait fini son travail, il sortait. | As soon as he had finished work, he would go out.

## si clauses: the three patterns

The verb after **si** (if) is never in the future or conditional. The main clause follows:

| si + … | Main clause | Example |
| ------ | ----------- | ------- |
| présent | futur / présent / imperative | Si tu viens, on ira au cinéma. |
| imparfait | conditionnel présent | Si j’avais le temps, je voyagerais. |
| plus-que-parfait | conditionnel passé | Si j’avais su, je serais venu. |
| plus-que-parfait | conditionnel présent (result now) | Si tu avais pris ton parapluie, tu ne serais pas mouillé. |

> [!TIP]
> **si** meaning *whether* (in an indirect question) is different: it **can** be followed by the future or the conditional. *Je me demande **s’il viendra**.* *Je me demandais **s’il viendrait**.*

## Subjunctive clauses

When the subordinate needs the **subjunctive**, modern French only uses two tenses, whatever the main verb’s tense:

| Subordinate action | Tense | Example |
| ------------------ | ----- | ------- |
| same time or after | subjonctif présent | Je veux / Je voulais / Je voudrais qu’il vienne. |
| before | subjonctif passé | Je suis / J’étais content qu’il soit venu. |

In classic literature you’ll also meet the **imparfait** and **plus-que-parfait du subjonctif** after a past main verb: *Je voulais qu’il **vînt***, *Je doutais qu’il **eût** compris*. Recognize them; don’t use them.

## A checklist

1. Find the main verb: present/future or past?
2. Is the subordinate action before, simultaneous or after?
3. Does the conjunction or verb need the subjunctive (vouloir que, bien que, avant que…)?
4. Is it a si-clause (if) or an indirect question (whether)?
5. Is it a general truth that can stay in the present?

## Quick summary

- Main present/future: passé composé (before), présent (same), futur (after).
- Main past: plus-que-parfait (before), imparfait (same), conditionnel (after), conditionnel passé (completed later).
- quand + future when the time is future.
- si + présent / imparfait / plus-que-parfait. Never si + futur or conditionnel (except si = whether).
- Subjunctive: présent (same/after) or passé (before), whatever the main tense.

## Goals

- present-main: Choose the tense after a main verb in the present or future § Main verb in the present or future
- past-main: Shift tenses after a main verb in the past § Main verb in the past
- truths: Keep general truths in the present § When the present stays: general truths
