## A tense to recognize, not to use

The **passé simple** is the past tense of written narration: novels, fairy tales, biographies, history. It means the same as the passé composé (*il parla* = *il a parlé*, he spoke), but you’ll almost never hear it in conversation.

For the DELF B2 and for reading French literature, you need to **recognize** it instantly, mostly in the **il / elle** and **ils / elles** forms. You don’t need to write it.

- Il entra, regarda autour de lui et sortit. | He came in, looked around and went out.
- Victor Hugo naquit en 1802. | Victor Hugo was born in 1802.

## Regular endings

| Pronoun | -er (parler) | -ir / -re (finir, attendre) |
| ------- | ------------ | --------------------------- |
| je      | parlai       | finis, attendis             |
| tu      | parlas       | finis                       |
| il / elle | **parla**  | **finit**, **attendit**     |
| nous    | parlâmes     | finîmes                     |
| vous    | parlâtes     | finîtes                     |
| ils / elles | **parlèrent** | **finirent**, **attendirent** |

> [!TIP]
> Endings to look for: **-a / -èrent** for -er verbs, **-it / -irent** for most others, **-ut / -urent** for many irregulars. If you see *-èrent*, *-irent* or *-urent*, you’re reading the passé simple.

## The irregulars you’ll meet most

| Infinitive | il / elle | ils / elles |
| ---------- | --------- | ----------- |
| être | fut | furent |
| avoir | eut | eurent |
| faire | fit | firent |
| aller | alla | allèrent |
| venir | vint | vinrent |
| voir | vit | virent |
| prendre | prit | prirent |
| dire | dit | dirent |
| pouvoir | put | purent |
| vouloir | voulut | voulurent |
| savoir | sut | surent |
| mettre | mit | mirent |
| naître | naquit | naquirent |
| mourir | mourut | moururent |

> [!WARNING]
> **il fut** is *être* (he was), not *faire*. **il fit** is *faire*. And **il vit** can be *voir* (passé simple: he saw) or *vivre* (present: he lives). The context decides.

## Passé simple and imparfait in a story

In written narration, the passé simple plays the role the passé composé plays in speech: it moves the story forward with events. The **imparfait** still sets the scene.

- Il faisait nuit. La maison était silencieuse. Soudain, quelqu’un frappa à la porte. | It was night. The house was silent. Suddenly someone knocked at the door.

## Full tables: être, avoir, faire, venir

You’ll mostly see the third person, but stories told in the first person use all the forms:

| | être | avoir | faire | venir |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| je | fus | eus | fis | vins |
| tu | fus | eus | fis | vins |
| il / elle | fut | eut | fit | vint |
| nous | fûmes | eûmes | fîmes | vînmes |
| vous | fûtes | eûtes | fîtes | vîntes |
| ils / elles | furent | eurent | firent | vinrent |

Two set phrases you’ll meet again and again: *Ce **fut** un très beau spectacle* (It was a wonderful show) and the fairy-tale ending *Ils **vécurent** heureux et **eurent** beaucoup d’enfants* (They lived happily ever after).

## The passé antérieur

The **passé antérieur** is the literary partner of the passé simple: **avoir / être in the passé simple + past participle**. It marks an action completed just **before** another one in the passé simple, mostly after *quand, lorsque, dès que, après que, à peine… que*:

- Quand il eut fini son repas, il sortit. | When he had finished his meal, he went out.
- Dès qu’elle fut partie, la fête commença. | As soon as she had left, the party began.
- À peine fut-il arrivé qu’il repartit. | No sooner had he arrived than he left again.

In speech and ordinary writing, use the **plus-que-parfait** (*quand il avait fini*) or the passé composé instead.

## Quick summary

- Literary past = passé composé in meaning. Recognize it; don’t use it in speech.
- -er: il parla, ils parlèrent. -ir/-re: il finit, ils finirent. Irregulars often in -ut / -urent.
- fut (être), eut (avoir), fit (faire), vint (venir), naquit (naître).
- Passé antérieur (eut fini, fut parti): an action just before another passé simple.
