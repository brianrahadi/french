## English “-ing” has many French translations

English uses “-ing” everywhere. French splits it into several structures, and the **present participle** (*parlant*) is much rarer than English “speaking”. Before reaching for *-ant*, check which job the “-ing” is doing:

| English              | French                               | Example                                |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| I am speaking        | present tense                        | je parle                               |
| I like cooking       | infinitive                           | j’aime cuisiner                        |
| before leaving       | avant de + infinitive                | avant de partir                        |
| without saying       | sans + infinitive                    | sans dire                              |
| after eating         | après avoir/être + participle        | après avoir mangé                      |
| while / by working   | gérondif: en + -ant                  | en travaillant                         |
| a girl singing       | qui + verb (or participle)           | une fille qui chante                   |

## Forming the present participle

Take the **nous** form of the present, remove **-ons**, add **-ant**:

- parler → nous parlons → parlant
- finir → nous finissons → finissant
- faire → nous faisons → faisant
- prendre → nous prenons → prenant
- voir → nous voyons → voyant

Three irregular ones: **être → étant, avoir → ayant, savoir → sachant**.

## The gérondif: en + present participle

The **gérondif** (*en parlant*) describes an action done by the **same subject** as the main verb. It expresses:

**Simultaneity** (while, when):

- Il écoute de la musique en travaillant. | He listens to music while working.
- Je l’ai rencontré en sortant du métro. | I met him as I was coming out of the metro.
- Elle est tombée en courant. | She fell while running.

**Manner or means** (by, through):

- C’est en lisant qu’on apprend. | It’s by reading that you learn.
- Il a réussi en travaillant dur. | He succeeded by working hard.
- Elle est entrée en courant. | She ran in. (came in running)

**Condition** (if):

- En partant tôt, tu éviteras les bouchons. | If you leave early, you’ll avoid traffic.
- En cherchant bien, tu trouveras. | If you look carefully, you’ll find it.

Adding **tout** stresses simultaneity or contrast: ***Tout en** étant malade, il est venu travailler* (Even though he was ill, he came to work).

> [!WARNING]
> The gérondif must refer to the **subject**. *J’ai vu Marie en sortant* means **I** was coming out, not Marie. If it was Marie, say *J’ai vu Marie **qui** sortait*.

## The present participle without en

Used alone, the present participle is mostly written and formal. It doesn’t agree, and it replaces:

**A qui-clause** (describing a noun):

- Nous cherchons un vendeur parlant espagnol. | We’re looking for a salesperson who speaks Spanish.
- Les personnes souhaitant participer doivent s’inscrire. | People wishing to take part must register.

**A cause** (as, since):

- Étant fatigué, il s’est couché tôt. | Being tired, he went to bed early.
- Ne sachant pas quoi dire, je me suis tu. | Not knowing what to say, I kept quiet.

## The perfect participle: ayant / étant + participle

For an action **completed before** the main one (“having done”):

- Ayant fini son travail, elle est sortie. | Having finished her work, she went out.
- Étant arrivés en retard, ils ont raté le début. | Having arrived late, they missed the beginning.

In everyday French, **après + past infinitive** is more common: ***Après avoir fini**, elle est sortie. **Après être arrivés**…*

## Verbal adjectives

Some -ant forms are also **adjectives**. Then they agree, and sometimes the spelling differs:

- une histoire intéressante, des films amusants | an interesting story, funny films
- une femme charmante | a charming woman
- fatiguant (participle) / fatigant (adjective): *un travail fatigant* | a tiring job
- convainquant / convaincant: *un argument convaincant* | a convincing argument

## Quick summary

- Present participle: nous-stem + -ant (étant, ayant, sachant).
- Gérondif (en + -ant): while, by, if. Same subject as the main verb.
- Alone, -ant replaces qui + verb or a cause (written style).
- After doing → après avoir/être + participle. Like doing → infinitive.
