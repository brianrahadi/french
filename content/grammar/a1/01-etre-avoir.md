## Subject pronouns

être (to be) and avoir (to have) are irregular, so learn them as full tables with their pronouns. They also build the passé composé later: *j’**ai** mangé*, *je **suis** parti*.

| Singular  |                          | Plural      |                         |
| --------- | ------------------------ | ----------- | ----------------------- |
| je (j’)   | I                        | nous        | we                      |
| tu        | you (informal)           | vous        | you (plural / formal)   |
| il / elle | he, it / she, it         | ils / elles | they (m. or mixed / f.) |
| on        | we (spoken), one, people |             |                         |

- **je** loses its e before a vowel or a silent h: *j’ai, j’arrive, j’habite*. Unlike English “I”, it has no capital letter except at the start of a sentence.
- **il / elle** also mean **it**: every noun is masculine or feminine, so a table is *elle* and a book is *il*: *La table ? Elle est dans la cuisine.*
- **ils** covers any group with at least one masculine noun or person, even 99 women and 1 man. **elles** is only for all-feminine groups.

## tu, vous and on

- Tu es prêt ? | Are you ready? (to a friend)
- Vous êtes prêt, monsieur ? | Are you ready, sir? (polite, one person)
- Vous êtes prêts, les enfants ? | Are you ready, kids? (a group)
- On est en retard ! | We’re late!
- Ici, on parle français. | French is spoken here. / People speak French here.

**tu** is for friends, family, children, pets and people your age in casual settings. **vous** is for strangers, shop staff, colleagues you don’t know well, anyone older or senior, and any group of people. When in doubt, use **vous**; the other person may suggest *On se tutoie ?* (Shall we say tu?).

**on** is everywhere in speech. It means “we” far more often than **nous**, and also “people / you / one” in general statements. It always takes the **il / elle** form of the verb, even when it means “we”: *on **est**, on **a***.

> [!TIP]
> In writing, an adjective after *on* = “we” often agrees with the real people: *On est contents* (we’re happy, a group). The verb never changes: always *on est*.

## Être: to be

|                | être   | pronunciation |
| -------------- | ------ | ------------- |
| je             | suis   | swee          |
| tu             | es     | eh            |
| il / elle / on | est    | eh            |
| nous           | sommes | som           |
| vous           | êtes   | et            |
| ils / elles    | sont   | sõ            |

Use **être** for identity, nationality, profession, description, location and time:

- Je suis canadien. / Elle est canadienne. | I’m Canadian. / She’s Canadian.
- Il est médecin. | He is a doctor.
- Nous sommes fatigués. | We are tired.
- Vous êtes au bureau ? | Are you at the office?
- Il est huit heures. | It’s eight o’clock.
- C’est mon frère. | This is my brother.

> [!WARNING]
> With a profession straight after être, French uses **no article**: *Il est ~~un~~ médecin* → **Il est médecin**. But with *c’est*, you need one: **C’est un** médecin. And as soon as there’s an adjective: *C’est **un** bon médecin.*

## Avoir: to have

|                | avoir | pronunciation |
| -------------- | ----- | ------------- |
| j’             | ai    | jay           |
| tu             | as    | ta (a)        |
| il / elle / on | a     | a             |
| nous           | avons | nooz-avõ      |
| vous           | avez  | vooz-avay     |
| ils / elles    | ont   | eelz-õ        |

The liaison in *nous‿avons, vous‿avez, ils‿ont, elles‿ont* sounds like a **z**. It’s the only thing that tells **ils‿ont** (they have, z sound) apart from **ils sont** (they are, s sound). Say the two side by side.

- J’ai deux frères. | I have two brothers.
- Tu as une voiture ? | Do you have a car?
- Ils ont un chat et un chien. | They have a cat and a dog.

## Where English says “be” but French says “have”

For these, French uses **avoir** + a noun:

| French                      | English               |
| --------------------------- | --------------------- |
| avoir 20 ans                | to be 20 (years old)  |
| avoir faim / soif           | to be hungry / thirsty |
| avoir chaud / froid         | to be hot / cold      |
| avoir peur (de)             | to be afraid (of)     |
| avoir raison / tort         | to be right / wrong   |
| avoir sommeil               | to be sleepy          |
| avoir besoin de             | to need               |
| avoir envie de              | to feel like, want    |
| avoir mal (à)               | to hurt, have a pain  |
| avoir de la chance          | to be lucky           |

- J’ai 30 ans. | I am 30.
- Tu as faim ? On mange ? | Are you hungry? Shall we eat?
- Elle a peur des chiens. | She’s afraid of dogs.
- Vous avez raison. | You’re right.
- J’ai besoin d’un café. | I need a coffee.
- J’ai mal à la tête. | I have a headache.

> [!WARNING]
> Never say ~~Je suis 30 ans~~ or ~~Je suis froid~~. *Je suis froid* means “I’m a cold person”. Use **avoir**: *J’ai froid*.

## Il y a: there is, there are

**il y a** (from avoir) means both “there is” and “there are”. It never changes for plural:

- Il y a un problème. | There’s a problem.
- Il y a beaucoup de monde. | There are a lot of people.
- Qu’est-ce qu’il y a ? | What’s the matter?
- Il n’y a pas de pain. | There’s no bread.

## Quick summary

- Pronouns: je, tu, il/elle/on, nous, vous, ils/elles. In speech on = we, with the il form.
- tu for friends and family, vous for strangers, seniors and groups.
- être: suis, es, est, sommes, êtes, sont. avoir: ai, as, a, avons, avez, ont.
- Age, hunger, thirst, heat, cold, fear, being right → **avoir**.
- il y a = there is / there are.

## Goals

- pronouns: Choose the right subject pronoun: tu or vous, on, ils or elles § tu, vous and on
- etre: Conjugate être in the present § Être: to be
- avoir: Conjugate avoir in the present § Avoir: to have
- avoir-expr: Use avoir for age, hunger, cold, fear and being right § Where English says “be” but French says “have”
- il-y-a: Say “there is / there are” with il y a § Il y a: there is, there are

## Exercises

### cloze
- sentence: Nous ___ étudiants.
- hint: être
- answer: sommes
- en: We are students.
- explain: nous → sommes.
- goal: etre

### cloze
- sentence: Tu ___ un chien ?
- hint: avoir
- answer: as
- en: Do you have a dog?
- explain: tu → as (the s is silent).
- goal: avoir

### cloze
- sentence: Ils ___ en retard.
- hint: être
- answer: sont
- en: They are late.
- explain: ils → sont. Don’t confuse with ils ont (they have).
- goal: etre

### cloze
- sentence: Elle ___ vingt ans.
- hint: avoir
- answer: a
- en: She is twenty.
- explain: Age uses avoir: elle a vingt ans.
- goal: avoir-expr

- explain: French uses avoir + ans for age.
- goal: avoir-expr
