## Three groups of verbs

French grammars sort verbs into three groups by the way they conjugate. Knowing a verb’s group tells you which endings to use, in the present and in other tenses.

| Group | Infinitive | Pattern | Examples |
| ----- | ---------- | ------- | -------- |
| 1st | -er | regular: parle, parlons | parler, aimer, habiter (about 90% of verbs) |
| 2nd | -ir, with **-iss-** in the plural | regular: finis, finissons | finir, choisir, réussir |
| 3rd | everything else | irregular, but in families | être, avoir, aller, faire, prendre, partir, attendre… |

The 3rd group sounds scary, but many of its verbs follow small patterns of their own. The most useful one is the **-re** pattern of *attendre*, which works like a regular group.

> [!WARNING]
> **aller** ends in -er but belongs to the 3rd group: *je vais, nous allons, ils vont*. It’s the only -er verb that breaks the pattern.

## Group 2: finir

Remove **-ir** and add **-is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent**. The **-iss-** in the plural marks the group.

|                | Ending  | finir      | choisir      | réussir      |
| -------------- | ------- | ---------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| je             | -is     | finis      | choisis      | réussis      |
| tu             | -is     | finis      | choisis      | réussis      |
| il / elle / on | -it     | finit      | choisit      | réussit      |
| nous           | -issons | finissons  | choisissons  | réussissons  |
| vous           | -issez  | finissez   | choisissez   | réussissez   |
| ils / elles    | -issent | finissent  | choisissent  | réussissent  |

- Je finis le travail à 18 heures. | I finish work at 6 pm.
- Vous choisissez quel dessert ? | Which dessert are you choosing?
- Les enfants grandissent vite. | Children grow up fast.
- Il réussit toujours ses examens. | He always passes his exams.

Many group 2 verbs come from adjectives and mean “to become …”: *grand → **grandir*** (grow up), *gros → **grossir*** (put on weight), *maigre → **maigrir*** (lose weight), *rouge → **rougir*** (blush), *vieux → **vieillir*** (get old), *pâle → **pâlir***.

Other common ones: *agir* (act), *obéir à* (obey), *réfléchir à* (think about), *remplir* (fill in), *punir* (punish), *nourrir* (feed), *atterrir* (land), *applaudir* (clap), *bâtir* (build), *guérir* (heal).

> [!TIP]
> **je finis, tu finis, il finit** all sound the same (“fee-nee”); you only hear the -ss- in the plural: *ils finissent* (“fee-niss”). That s-sound is how you hear “they” vs. “he”: *il finit* / *ils finissent*.

## -ir verbs that don’t follow finir

Some very common -ir verbs belong to the 3rd group. The test: if the nous form has no **-iss-**, it’s not group 2.

**partir, sortir, dormir, sentir, servir, mentir** drop the consonant before -ir in the singular:

|                | partir   | dormir   | sortir   |
| -------------- | -------- | -------- | -------- |
| je             | pars     | dors     | sors     |
| tu             | pars     | dors     | sors     |
| il / elle / on | part     | dort     | sort     |
| nous           | partons  | dormons  | sortons  |
| vous           | partez   | dormez   | sortez   |
| ils / elles    | partent  | dorment  | sortent  |

**ouvrir, offrir, découvrir, souffrir** take **-er endings**: *j’ouvre, tu ouvres, il ouvre, nous ouvrons, vous ouvrez, ils ouvrent*.

- Je pars demain matin. | I’m leaving tomorrow morning.
- Le bébé dort. | The baby is sleeping.
- Tu ouvres la fenêtre ? | Will you open the window?
- Je t’offre un café. | Let me buy you a coffee.

> [!WARNING]
> Never ~~nous partissons~~ or ~~nous dormissons~~. Only true group 2 verbs take -iss-: *nous finissons*, but *nous partons, nous dormons*.

## The -re pattern: attendre

Verbs in **-dre** (except prendre) follow one pattern: remove **-re** and add **-s, -s, (nothing), -ons, -ez, -ent**. The il form has no ending.

|                | Ending | attendre    | vendre    | répondre    |
| -------------- | ------ | ----------- | --------- | ----------- |
| je (j’)        | -s     | attends     | vends     | réponds     |
| tu             | -s     | attends     | vends     | réponds     |
| il / elle / on | —      | attend      | vend      | répond      |
| nous           | -ons   | attendons   | vendons   | répondons   |
| vous           | -ez    | attendez    | vendez    | répondez    |
| ils / elles    | -ent   | attendent   | vendent   | répondent   |

Same pattern: *entendre* (hear), *descendre* (go down), *perdre* (lose), *rendre* (give back), *défendre*, *tondre*, *mordre*.

- J’attends le bus. | I’m waiting for the bus.
- Il vend sa voiture. | He’s selling his car.
- Vous entendez ce bruit ? | Can you hear that noise?
- Elles descendent à la prochaine station. | They get off at the next stop.

The **d** is silent in the singular (*il attend* = “a-tã”) and pronounced in the plural (*ils attendent* = “a-tãd”). In inversion it sounds like a t: *Attend-il ?* (“a-tã-teel”).

> [!WARNING]
> **prendre** looks like a -dre verb but isn’t: *nous **prenons**, ils **prennent*** (not ~~nous prendons~~). The same goes for *apprendre* and *comprendre*.

## One present, several English meanings

French has no “-ing” tense. One French form covers several English ones, and English continuous tenses map onto ordinary French tenses:

| English | French |
| ------- | ------ |
| I finish / I am finishing / I do finish | je finis |
| I’m (busy) finishing, stressing *right now* | je suis en train de finir |
| I have been waiting for an hour | j’attends depuis une heure |
| I was waiting (when…) | j’attendais (quand…) |
| I will be waiting | j’attendrai |

- J’attends depuis vingt minutes. | I’ve been waiting for twenty minutes.
- Qu’est-ce que tu fais ? — Je finis mes devoirs. | What are you doing? — I’m finishing my homework.

## The infinitive: the verb’s name

The infinitive (*finir, attendre, parler*) is the form in the dictionary. Besides following another verb (*j’aime lire, je veux partir*), it’s used:

- **after prepositions**, where English often uses -ing: *pour réussir* (in order to succeed), *sans répondre* (without answering), *avant de partir* (before leaving).
- **as the subject** of a sentence: ***Marcher** est bon pour la santé* (Walking is good for your health).
- **in instructions, recipes and signs**: *Ajouter le sucre. Ne pas fumer.*
- **in short questions**: *Que faire ?* (What should we do?), *Où aller ?*

- Il part sans dire au revoir. | He leaves without saying goodbye.
- Lire avant de dormir, c’est mon plaisir. | Reading before sleeping is my pleasure.

## Quick summary

- Group 1 (-er): parle, parlons. Group 2 (-ir + -iss-): finis, finissons. Group 3: the rest.
- finir: -is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent.
- partir, sortir, dormir: je pars, nous partons (no -iss-).
- attendre: -s, -s, (nothing), -ons, -ez, -ent (il attend). But prendre → nous prenons.
- No -ing in French: j’attends = I wait / I’m waiting; j’attends depuis une heure = I’ve been waiting an hour.

## Goals

- finir: Conjugate group 2 verbs like finir (-issons) § Group 2: finir
- partir: Conjugate partir, dormir, sortir and ouvrir § -ir verbs that don’t follow finir
- attendre: Conjugate -re verbs like attendre (il attend) § The -re pattern: attendre
- no-ing: Translate English -ing: present, depuis, infinitive § One present, several English meanings

- explain: Group 2: nous + -issons → finissons.
- goal: finir
