## Every noun has a gender

French nouns are either **masculine** or **feminine** — even objects and ideas: *le livre* (m), *la table* (f), *le courage* (m), *la liberté* (f). There’s no “it”-gender. The gender decides the article, the adjectives, the pronouns and past-participle agreement, so a wrong gender ripples through the whole sentence.

The golden rule: **always learn a noun with its article**. Not *maison* but *la maison*; not *ami* but *un ami / une amie*. For nouns starting with a vowel, where *l’* hides the gender, learn them with *un / une*: *un hôtel, une école*.

## Definite articles — the

|                           | Singular                | Plural     |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------- |
| Masculine                 | le livre                | les livres |
| Feminine                  | la table                | les tables |
| Before a vowel / silent h | l’ami, l’école, l’hôtel | les‿amis   |

French uses the definite article much more than English. Use it for:

- **Specific** things both people know: *le livre de Paul, la voiture rouge.*
- Things **in general** and categories: *Le sport est bon pour la santé* (Sport is good for your health).
- **Likes and dislikes** — aimer, adorer, préférer, détester always take le/la/les: *J’aime **le** chocolat*, *Je déteste **les** araignées*.
- Languages, school subjects, countries: *le français, les maths, la France.*
- Days for habits: *Le lundi, je travaille* (On Mondays I work) vs. *Lundi, je travaille* (This Monday).

- Les enfants aiment les bonbons. | Children like sweets.
- La vie est belle. | Life is beautiful.
- Elle parle le chinois. | She speaks Chinese.

## Indefinite articles — a, an, some

|           | Singular  | Plural     |
| --------- | --------- | ---------- |
| Masculine | un livre  | des livres |
| Feminine  | une table | des tables |

**un / une** = a, an. **des** is the plural — “some”, or often nothing in English. French almost never leaves a noun bare, so **des** appears where English has no word at all:

- J’ai des amis à Paris. | I have friends in Paris.
- Il y a des enfants dans le jardin. | There are children in the garden.
- Tu veux des frites ? | Do you want (some) fries?

> [!TIP]
> Before an adjective placed in front of a plural noun, written French turns **des** into **de**: *des fleurs* but ***de** belles fleurs*. In speech you’ll hear both.

## Le or un? Choosing the article

- Un chien aboie. | A dog is barking. (one dog, not identified)
- Le chien aboie. | The dog is barking. (the one we know)
- J’aime les chiens. | I like dogs. (dogs in general)
- J’ai des chiens. | I have dogs. (some dogs)

> [!WARNING]
> “I like dogs” is **J’aime les chiens**, never ~~J’aime des chiens~~ — likes take the definite article.

## Clues to guess the gender

Endings are reliable clues (about 80–90% of the time):

| Usually feminine           | Usually masculine        |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| -tion, -sion (la nation)   | -ment (le moment)        |
| -té (la liberté)           | -age (le fromage)        |
| -ure (la voiture)          | -eau (le bateau)         |
| -ette (la baguette)        | -isme (le tourisme)      |
| -ence, -ance (la chance)   | -oir (le miroir)         |
| -ie (la boulangerie)       | -et (le jouet)           |
| -ée (la journée)           | -ier (le cahier)         |
| -ude (l’habitude)          | consonant ending (le lit) |

Other helpful patterns: days, months, seasons, languages, trees and metals are masculine (*le lundi, le printemps, le français, le chêne, l’or*). Most countries and fruits ending in -e are feminine (*la France, la pomme*).

> [!WARNING]
> Common exceptions: **la** plage, **la** page, **la** cage, **l’eau** (f), **la** peau, **la** fin, **la** mer, **la** main — and **le** problème, **le** système, **le** musée, **le** lycée.

## Male and female people

Many nouns for people have two forms: *un ami / une amie, un étudiant / une étudiante, un acteur / une actrice, un boulanger / une boulangère, un musicien / une musicienne*. Some are identical with only the article changing: *un / une élève, un / une collègue, un / une journaliste*.

## Plurals

- Most nouns add a silent **-s**: *un chat → des chats*. You hear the plural only in the article (le/les, un/des).
- **-eau / -eu → -x**: *un gâteau → des gâteaux*, *un jeu → des jeux*.
- **-al → -aux**: *un journal → des journaux*, *un animal → des animaux*.
- **-ou → -s**, except seven that take -x: *bijou, caillou, chou, genou, hibou, joujou, pou*.
- Nouns ending in -s, -x, -z don’t change: *un pays → des pays, un prix → des prix*.
- Irregular: *un œil → des yeux*, *monsieur → messieurs*, *madame → mesdames*.

## Quick summary

- Learn every noun with its article.
- le / la / l’ / les = the — also for general statements and after aimer.
- un / une / des = a, some — des where English has nothing.
- Endings like -tion, -té, -ure are feminine; -ment, -age, -eau are masculine.
