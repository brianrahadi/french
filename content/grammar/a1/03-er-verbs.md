## The -er group

About 90% of French verbs end in **-er**, and almost all of them follow one pattern. New verbs join this group too (*googler, liker, scanner*). Common ones: *parler, aimer, habiter, travailler, regarder, écouter, chercher, donner, jouer…*

## Stem + ending

Remove **-er** from the infinitive to get the stem (*parler → parl-*), then add the endings:

|                | Ending | parler  | aimer  | habiter   |
| -------------- | ------ | ------- | ------ | --------- |
| je (j’)        | -e     | parle   | aime   | habite    |
| tu             | -es    | parles  | aimes  | habites   |
| il / elle / on | -e     | parle   | aime   | habite    |
| nous           | -ons   | parlons | aimons | habitons  |
| vous           | -ez    | parlez  | aimez  | habitez   |
| ils / elles    | -ent   | parlent | aiment | habitent  |

> [!TIP]
> **parle, parles, parle, parlent** sound the same: the -e, -es and -ent endings are silent. Only *parlons* and *parlez* sound different, so in speech there are three forms.

> [!WARNING]
> Never pronounce the **-ent** of *ils parlent*. It’s silent: “eel parl”. (The -ent is only pronounced in other words, like *le moment*.)

## Before a vowel: j’ and liaison

**je** becomes **j’** before a vowel or silent h: *j’aime, j’écoute, j’habite*. In the plural, there’s a liaison with a **z** sound: *nous‿aimons, vous‿écoutez, ils‿habitent, elles‿arrivent*.

## One tense, three English meanings

French has no separate continuous tense. *Je parle* means **I speak**, **I’m speaking** and **I do speak**. Context does the work.

- Nous habitons à Lyon. | We live in Lyon.
- Qu’est-ce que tu regardes ? | What are you watching?
- Ils travaillent le samedi. | They work on Saturdays.
- J’étudie le français depuis deux ans. | I’ve been studying French for two years.

To stress that something is happening right now, use **être en train de** + infinitive: *Je suis en train de cuisiner* (I’m (busy) cooking). The present with **depuis** expresses something that started in the past and is still going on, where English uses “have been doing”.

## Spelling changes to keep the sound

A few groups of -er verbs change their spelling to keep the pronunciation regular:

| Verb type            | Change                | Example                                  |
| -------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| -ger                 | nous + e              | manger → nous mangeons                   |
| -cer                 | nous: c → ç           | commencer → nous commençons              |
| e + consonant + er   | e → è (not nous/vous) | acheter → j’achète, ils achètent         |
| é + consonant + er   | é → è (not nous/vous) | préférer → je préfère, ils préfèrent     |
| -eler / -eter (most) | double consonant      | appeler → j’appelle; jeter → je jette    |
| -oyer / -uyer        | y → i                 | nettoyer → je nettoie; ennuyer → il ennuie |

The **-ger / -cer** changes only affect *nous* (before -ons, a hard vowel). All the others happen when the ending is **silent** (je, tu, il, ils). The nous and vous forms keep the infinitive’s spelling: *nous achetons, vous préférez, nous appelons*.

Verbs ending in **-ayer** can do either: *je paie* or *je paye*.

## The one irregular: aller

**aller** (to go) ends in -er but is completely irregular: *je vais, tu vas, il va, nous allons, vous allez, ils vont*. Every other -er verb follows the patterns above.

## Useful -er verbs to practise with

- aimer, adorer, détester, préférer | like, love, hate, prefer
- parler, écouter, regarder, chercher | speak, listen, watch, look for
- habiter, travailler, étudier, jouer | live, work, study, play
- manger, acheter, payer, commencer | eat, buy, pay, start
- arriver, rentrer, rester, tomber | arrive, come home, stay, fall

## Quick summary

- Stem + -e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent.
- Four of the six forms sound the same.
- je parle = I speak / I’m speaking.
- Watch the spelling changes: mangeons, commençons, achète, appelle, nettoie.

## Goals

- endings: Conjugate regular -er verbs in the present § Stem + ending
- one-tense: Use one present for “I speak” and “I’m speaking” § One tense, three English meanings
