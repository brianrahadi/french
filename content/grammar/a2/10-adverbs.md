## What adverbs do

An adverb tells you **how, when, where, how often** or **how much**. It modifies a verb, an adjective or another adverb, and it **never changes**: no -e, no -s.

| Type | Examples |
| ---- | -------- |
| manner | bien, mal, vite, lentement, ensemble, ainsi |
| time | hier, aujourd’hui, demain, déjà, bientôt, tôt, tard, encore, maintenant |
| frequency | toujours, souvent, parfois, rarement, jamais |
| place | ici, là, là-bas, partout, ailleurs, dehors, dedans, loin, près |
| quantity / degree | très, trop, assez, beaucoup, peu, tellement, presque, plutôt |

- Elle parle très bien. | She speaks very well.
- Il conduit trop vite. | He drives too fast.
- Les enfants jouent dehors. | The children are playing outside.

## Building adverbs in -ment

Most adverbs of manner are built from the **feminine** adjective + **-ment** (English -ly):

| Adjective (m / f) | Adverb |
| ----------------- | ------ |
| lent / lente | lentement |
| sérieux / sérieuse | sérieusement |
| heureux / heureuse | heureusement |
| doux / douce | doucement |
| léger / légère | légèrement |
| discret / discrète | discrètement |
| actif / active | activement |

If the **masculine** ends in a vowel, add -ment to it directly: *vrai → vraiment, poli → poliment, facile → facilement, rapide → rapidement, absolu → absolument*.

Adjectives in **-ant** and **-ent** replace it with **-amment / -emment**, both pronounced “-amã”:

| -ant → -amment | -ent → -emment |
| -------------- | -------------- |
| courant → couramment | évident → évidemment |
| constant → constamment | récent → récemment |
| bruyant → bruyamment | patient → patiemment |
| brillant → brillamment | prudent → prudemment |

> [!WARNING]
> A few have their own spelling: *gentil → **gentiment***, *bref → **brièvement***, and some take **-ément**: *précis → précisément, énorme → énormément, profond → profondément*. And **lent → lentement** (not ~~lemment~~): only -ent adjectives take -emment.

## The irregular basics

The most common adverbs don’t end in -ment at all:

| Adjective | Adverb |
| --------- | ------ |
| bon (good) | **bien** (well) |
| mauvais (bad) | **mal** (badly) |
| meilleur (better) | **mieux** (better) |
| rapide (quick) | **vite** or rapidement (quickly) |

- C’est un bon cuisinier : il cuisine bien. | He’s a good cook: he cooks well.
- J’ai mal dormi. | I slept badly.

Some adjectives are used **as adverbs** in fixed expressions, and then they don’t agree: *parler **fort** / **bas*** (loudly / quietly), *coûter **cher***, *travailler **dur***, *sentir **bon** / **mauvais***, *chanter **faux***, *voir **clair***.

- Ces fleurs sentent bon. | These flowers smell nice.
- Les fraises coûtent cher en hiver. | Strawberries are expensive in winter.
- Parlez plus fort, s’il vous plaît. | Speak up, please.

## Position: simple tenses

With a conjugated verb, the adverb goes **right after the verb**, never between the subject and the verb as in English:

- Je mange souvent au restaurant. | I often eat at the restaurant.
- Il parle bien français. | He speaks French well.
- Nous partons demain. | We’re leaving tomorrow.

Before an **adjective** or another **adverb**, it goes in front, as in English: *très gentil, trop cher, assez souvent, vraiment bien*.

> [!WARNING]
> ~~Je souvent mange~~ / ~~Il bien parle~~: in French, nothing comes between the subject and the verb except ne and object pronouns.

## Position: the passé composé

This is where French differs most from English:

- **Short, common adverbs** go **between the auxiliary and the participle**: *bien, mal, déjà, encore, toujours, souvent, jamais, trop, beaucoup, assez, peu, vite, presque, enfin, vraiment*.
- **Time words** naming a day or moment, and **place** adverbs, go **after the participle**: *hier, aujourd’hui, demain, tôt, tard, ici, là-bas, partout, ailleurs*.
- **Longer -ment adverbs** usually go after the participle too.

| Adverb | Example | Meaning |
| ------ | ------- | ------- |
| bien | J’ai bien dormi. | I slept well. |
| déjà | Tu as déjà mangé ? | Have you already eaten? |
| beaucoup | Il a beaucoup travaillé. | He worked a lot. |
| souvent | J’ai souvent joué au tennis. | I often played tennis. |
| hier | Je l’ai vu hier. | I saw him yesterday. |
| ailleurs | Il est parti ailleurs. | He went somewhere else. |
| poliment | Elle a répondu poliment. | She answered politely. |

With **verb + infinitive**, short adverbs usually go before the infinitive: *Je veux bien comprendre. Il faut toujours vérifier.*

## Quantity adverbs + de

**beaucoup, trop, assez, peu, tellement, plus, moins, autant** + noun take **de** (d’ before a vowel) with no article:

- J’ai beaucoup d’amis. | I have a lot of friends.
- Il boit trop de café. | He drinks too much coffee.
- Tu as assez d’argent ? | Do you have enough money?
- Je mange peu de viande. | I don’t eat much meat.

## Sentence adverbs

Some adverbs comment on the whole sentence. They usually open it, followed by a comma: *heureusement* (luckily), *malheureusement* (unfortunately), *évidemment* (obviously), *apparemment* (apparently), *franchement* (honestly), *d’ailleurs* (besides), *bien sûr* (of course).

- Heureusement, il ne pleuvait pas. | Luckily, it wasn’t raining.
- Franchement, je ne sais pas. | Honestly, I don’t know.

## Quick summary

- Adverbs never agree.
- feminine adjective + -ment: lentement; vowel + -ment: vraiment; -ant/-ent → -amment/-emment.
- bon → bien, mauvais → mal, meilleur → mieux; parler fort, coûter cher.
- After the verb: je mange souvent.
- Passé composé: j’ai bien dormi, tu as déjà mangé, but je l’ai vu hier.
- beaucoup de, trop de, assez de + noun.

- goal: ment

- goal: ment
