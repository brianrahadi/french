## Two tenses built from verbs you already know

You don’t need a new set of endings to talk about the near future or the recent past. You just need **aller** and **venir** in the present, plus an **infinitive**. That’s why these are the first “other times” French learners master — and why native speakers use them constantly.

## Futur proche: aller + infinitive

|             | aller | + infinitive |
| ----------- | ----- | ------------ |
| je          | vais  | partir       |
| tu          | vas   | manger       |
| il/elle/on  | va    | pleuvoir     |
| nous        | allons | voyager     |
| vous        | allez | voir         |
| ils/elles   | vont  | arriver      |

It corresponds to English “going to”:

- Je vais appeler Marie ce soir. | I’m going to call Marie tonight.
- Attention, tu vas tomber ! | Careful, you’re going to fall!
- Il va pleuvoir. | It’s going to rain.
- On va voir. | We’ll see.
- Qu’est-ce que vous allez faire demain ? | What are you going to do tomorrow?

In spoken French the futur proche is used for **almost any future**, near or far, especially plans and intentions: *L’année prochaine, je vais étudier à Paris.* The futur simple (*j’étudierai*) is more common in writing, formal speech, and for promises and predictions.

> [!TIP]
> *aller aller* is perfectly correct: ***Je vais aller** à la plage* (I’m going to go to the beach). But you can also just say *Je vais à la plage demain* — the present often works for scheduled plans.

## Futur proche: where things go

- **Negative**: ne … pas goes around **aller**: *Je **ne** vais **pas** partir.*
- **Pronouns** go before the **infinitive** (the verb they belong to): *Je vais **le** faire. Tu vas **lui** téléphoner ? Je vais **me** coucher.*
- **Questions**: *Tu vas venir ? Est-ce que tu vas venir ? Vas-tu venir ?*

- Je ne vais pas le faire. | I’m not going to do it.
- Elle va se marier en juin. | She’s going to get married in June.
- On ne va pas y aller. | We’re not going to go (there).

## Passé récent: venir de + infinitive

|             | venir | + de + infinitive |
| ----------- | ----- | ----------------- |
| je          | viens | de finir          |
| tu          | viens | de manger         |
| il/elle/on  | vient | d’arriver         |
| nous        | venons | de voir          |
| vous        | venez | de partir         |
| ils/elles   | viennent | de rentrer     |

It means “to have just done” something:

- Je viens de finir mon travail. | I’ve just finished my work.
- Ils viennent d’arriver. | They’ve just arrived.
- Le train vient de partir. | The train has just left.
- On vient de se rencontrer. | We’ve just met.

**de** becomes **d’** before a vowel: *Elle vient **d’**appeler.* Pronouns again go before the infinitive: *Je viens **de le** voir* (I’ve just seen him) — note that *de* and *le* do **not** contract here.

> [!WARNING]
> Don’t confuse *venir de + place* (to come from) with *venir de + infinitive* (to have just): *Je viens de Lyon* (I’m from Lyon) vs. *Je viens de manger* (I’ve just eaten).

## Être en train de: right now

To complete the set, **être en train de** + infinitive stresses an action in progress:

- Je suis en train de cuisiner. | I’m (busy) cooking.
- Ne le dérange pas, il est en train de travailler. | Don’t disturb him, he’s working.

| Just done          | In progress                 | About to happen          |
| ------------------ | --------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Je viens de manger. | Je suis en train de manger. | Je vais manger.          |
| I’ve just eaten.   | I’m eating.                 | I’m going to eat.        |

You can also say “about to” with **être sur le point de**: *Le film est sur le point de commencer.*

## Quick summary

- Near future: aller (present) + infinitive — je vais partir.
- Recent past: venir de (present) + infinitive — je viens de partir.
- Negative around aller/venir; pronouns before the infinitive.
- être en train de = be in the middle of doing.
