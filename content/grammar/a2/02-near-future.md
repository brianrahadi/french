## Futur proche: aller + infinitive

Conjugate **aller** in the present and add an infinitive. It’s the most common way to talk about the future in spoken French.

- Je vais manger. | I’m going to eat.
- Il va pleuvoir. | It’s going to rain.
- Nous allons partir demain. | We’re leaving tomorrow.

Negative: **ne … pas** goes around **aller**: *Je **ne** vais **pas** venir.*

## Passé récent: venir de + infinitive

**venir** in the present + **de** + infinitive means “to have just done”.

- Je viens de manger. | I’ve just eaten.
- Ils viennent d’arriver. | They’ve just arrived.
- Le film vient de commencer. | The film has just started.

> [!WARNING]
> Don’t forget **de**: ~~Je viens manger~~ means “I’m coming to eat”.

## Exercises

### transform
- instruction: Rewrite in the futur proche.
- source: Je mange.
- answer: Je vais manger

### transform
- instruction: Rewrite in the futur proche.
- source: Nous partons.
- answer: Nous allons partir

### cloze
- sentence: Attention, il ___ pleuvoir !
- answer: va
- en: Careful, it’s going to rain!
- explain: il va + infinitive.

### cloze
- sentence: Ils ___ arriver.
- hint: just
- answer: viennent d’
- answer: viennent de
- en: They’ve just arrived.
- explain: venir de → d’ before a vowel.

### cloze
- sentence: Je ___ finir mon travail.
- hint: just
- answer: viens de
- en: I’ve just finished my work.
- explain: venir de + infinitive = to have just done.

### mcq
- prompt: What does “Je viens de voir Paul” mean?
- [ ] I’m coming to see Paul.
- [x] I’ve just seen Paul.
- [ ] I’m going to see Paul.
- explain: venir de + infinitive = just did.

### transform
- instruction: Make it negative.
- source: Je vais sortir ce soir.
- answer: Je ne vais pas sortir ce soir
- explain: ne … pas surrounds aller.

### order

### order
