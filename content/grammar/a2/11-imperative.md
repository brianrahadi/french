## Three forms, no subject

The imperative is used for orders, instructions, advice and invitations. It has only three forms, **tu**, **nous** and **vous**, and no subject pronoun. Take the present tense and drop the pronoun.

| Present         | Imperative  | Meaning           |
| --------------- | ----------- | ----------------- |
| tu prends       | Prends !    | Take!             |
| nous prenons    | Prenons !   | Let’s take!       |
| vous prenez     | Prenez !    | Take! (polite/plural) |

> [!WARNING]
> **-er verbs drop the -s in the tu form**: *tu parles* → *Parle !*, *tu manges* → *Mange !* This includes **aller**: *Va !* Other verbs keep it: *Finis !*, *Prends !*, *Fais !*

- Regarde ! | Look!
- Allons au cinéma. | Let’s go to the cinema.
- Fermez la porte, s’il vous plaît. | Close the door, please.

## Irregular imperatives

Four verbs have their own forms. Learn them as a set:

| Verb    | tu     | nous     | vous     |
| ------- | ------ | -------- | -------- |
| être    | sois   | soyons   | soyez    |
| avoir   | aie    | ayons    | ayez     |
| savoir  | sache  | sachons  | sachez   |
| vouloir | veuille | —       | veuillez |

- Sois sage ! | Be good!
- N’ayez pas peur. | Don’t be afraid.
- Veuillez patienter. | Please wait. (very formal)

## Negative imperative

Put **ne… pas** around the verb, as usual:

- Ne parle pas ! | Don’t speak!
- Ne mangez pas ici. | Don’t eat here.
- N’oublions pas. | Let’s not forget.

## Pronouns with the imperative

In a **positive** order, object pronouns go **after** the verb, joined with a hyphen, and **me / te** become **moi / toi**:

- Prends-le. | Take it.
- Donne-moi ton numéro. | Give me your number.
- Assieds-toi. | Sit down.
- Parlez-lui. | Talk to him.
- Vas-y ! | Go on! / Go there!

In a **negative** order, everything goes back to normal order, before the verb:

- Ne le prends pas. | Don’t take it.
- Ne t’inquiète pas. | Don’t worry.
- Ne lui parlez pas. | Don’t talk to him.

> [!TIP]
> *Vas-y* and *Manges-en* get an **-s** back before **y** and **en**, to make the sound smoother. It’s the only time an -er verb keeps its -s in the tu form.

## Reflexive verbs in the imperative

Reflexive verbs keep their pronoun. In a **positive** order it goes after the verb, and **te** becomes **toi**; in a **negative** order it goes back before the verb:

| Verb | Positive | Negative |
| ---- | -------- | -------- |
| se lever (tu) | Lève-toi ! | Ne te lève pas ! |
| se dépêcher (nous) | Dépêchons-nous ! | Ne nous dépêchons pas ! |
| s’asseoir (vous) | Asseyez-vous ! | Ne vous asseyez pas ! |
| s’inquiéter (tu) | Inquiète-toi ! | Ne t’inquiète pas ! |

- Habille-toi, on part ! | Get dressed, we’re leaving!
- Amusez-vous bien ! | Have fun!
- Ne t’en fais pas. | Don’t worry about it.

## Softer than an order

A bare imperative can sound abrupt. To soften it, add **s’il te plaît / s’il vous plaît**, use **pouvoir** in the conditional, or turn it into a suggestion with **on**:

- Ferme la porte, s’il te plaît. | Close the door, please.
- Tu pourrais fermer la porte ? | Could you close the door?
- On y va ? | Shall we go?
- N’hésitez pas à m’appeler. | Feel free to call me.

Signs, recipes and instructions often use the **infinitive** instead: *Ne pas fumer. Mélanger la farine et le sucre.*

## Quick summary

- Imperative = present without the subject; tu form of -er verbs drops the -s (*Parle !*).
- Irregular: sois, aie, sache, veuillez.
- Positive: verb-pronoun (*Prends-le*, *Donne-moi*). Negative: *Ne le prends pas*.
- Reflexive: Lève-toi ! / Ne te lève pas !
- Soften with s’il te plaît, tu pourrais…, or on.
