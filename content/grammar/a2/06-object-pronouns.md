## Direct or indirect?

An object pronoun replaces a noun already mentioned: *Tu connais Paul ? — Oui, je **le** connais.* You choose between a **direct** (COD) and an **indirect** (COI) pronoun, and it goes **before the verb**.

Ask what follows the verb in a normal sentence:

- **Direct object (COD)**: the noun follows the verb **directly**, with no preposition: *Je vois **Paul***. *J’attends **le bus***.
- **Indirect object (COI)**: the noun is introduced by **à**: *Je parle **à Paul***. *J’écris **à ma mère***.

| Person      | Direct (COD)  | Indirect (COI) |
| ----------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| me          | me (m’)       | me (m’)        |
| you (tu)    | te (t’)       | te (t’)        |
| him / it    | le (l’)       | lui            |
| her / it    | la (l’)       | lui            |
| us          | nous          | nous           |
| you (vous)  | vous          | vous           |
| them        | les           | leur           |

Only the third person is different: **le, la, les** (direct) vs. **lui, leur** (indirect). **lui** means both “to him” and “to her”.

- Je vois Marie. → Je la vois. | I see her.
- J’aime ces chansons. → Je les aime. | I love them.
- Je parle à Marie. → Je lui parle. | I’m talking to her.
- J’écris à mes parents. → Je leur écris. | I write to them.

## Verbs that don’t match English

These verbs take a different kind of object in French than in English:

| Direct in French (no à)     | Indirect in French (+ à)        |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| attendre qqn (wait **for**) | téléphoner à qqn (phone)        |
| chercher qqn (look **for**) | répondre à qqn (answer)         |
| écouter qqn (listen **to**) | demander à qqn (ask)            |
| regarder qqn (look **at**)  | dire à qqn (tell)               |
| aider qqn (help)            | conseiller à qqn (advise)       |
| remercier qqn (thank)       | plaire à qqn (please, be liked) |

- J’attends Marie. → Je l’attends. | I’m waiting for her.
- Je téléphone à Marie. → Je lui téléphone. | I’m phoning her.
- Ce film plaît à mes enfants. → Ce film leur plaît. | My children like this film.

> [!WARNING]
> **leur** (to them) never takes an -s: *je **leur** parle*. Don’t confuse it with the possessive *leurs* (*leurs enfants*).

## Placement: before the conjugated verb

The pronoun goes directly **before the verb it belongs to**:

| Structure          | Example                    | Meaning                      |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| present            | Je le connais.             | I know him.                  |
| negative           | Je ne le connais pas.      | I don’t know him.            |
| question           | Le connais-tu ?            | Do you know him?             |
| passé composé      | Je l’ai vu.                | I saw him.                   |
| + infinitive       | Je vais le voir.           | I’m going to see him.        |
| modal + infinitive | Je peux lui parler ?       | Can I talk to him/her?       |

With **verb + infinitive** (aller, pouvoir, vouloir, devoir…), the pronoun goes before the **infinitive**, because it’s the object of that verb.

Two cases to watch:

- With **several verbs**, the pronoun goes before the one it belongs to: *Ils vont devoir **les** réviser* (They’ll have to revise them). *Je vais pouvoir **lui** parler.*
- With **faire** and **laisser** + infinitive, the pronoun jumps in front of faire / laisser: *Je **les** laisse jouer dehors* (I let them play outside). *Elle **le** fait rire* (She makes him laugh).

> [!TIP]
> **le, la** become **l’** before a vowel or silent h: *je **l’**aime, je **l’**ai vu*. **me, te** become **m’, t’**: *il **m’**appelle*.

## In the passé composé: agreement

With a direct pronoun before **avoir**, the past participle agrees with it:

- J’ai vu Marie. → Je l’ai vue. | I saw her.
- Tu as pris les clés ? — Oui, je les ai prises. | Did you take the keys? — Yes, I took them.
- J’ai parlé à Marie. → Je lui ai parlé. | I spoke to her. (indirect: no agreement)

## Imperative

In **positive** commands, the pronoun goes **after** the verb with a hyphen, and **me / te** become **moi / toi**. In **negative** commands, it goes back in front:

- Regarde-moi ! / Ne me regarde pas ! | Look at me! / Don’t look at me!
- Prends-le ! / Ne le prends pas ! | Take it! / Don’t take it!
- Téléphone-lui ! / Ne lui téléphone pas ! | Phone him/her! / Don’t phone him/her!
- Aidez-nous ! | Help us!

## Le as “it” for an idea

**le** can stand for a whole idea or sentence: *Je le sais* (I know it/that), *Je te l’ai dit* (I told you so), *Il est malade ? — Je ne le crois pas* (I don’t think so).

## Quick summary

- Direct: me, te, le/la, nous, vous, les. Indirect: me, te, lui, nous, vous, leur.
- Verb + à → indirect (lui, leur). No preposition → direct (le, la, les).
- Before the verb, or before the infinitive with verb + infinitive.
- Positive imperative: after, with moi/toi.
