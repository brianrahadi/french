## Formation: future stem + imparfait endings

The **conditional** combines what you already know: the **future stem** (always ending in r) + the **imparfait endings** (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient).

|                | parler      | être     | avoir    | pouvoir    |
| -------------- | ----------- | -------- | -------- | ---------- |
| je             | parlerais   | serais   | aurais   | pourrais   |
| tu             | parlerais   | serais   | aurais   | pourrais   |
| il / elle / on | parlerait   | serait   | aurait   | pourrait   |
| nous           | parlerions  | serions  | aurions  | pourrions  |
| vous           | parleriez   | seriez   | auriez   | pourriez   |
| ils / elles    | parleraient | seraient | auraient | pourraient |

All the irregular future stems apply: *je ferais, j’irais, je voudrais, je devrais, je saurais, je viendrais, je verrais, il faudrait*.

> [!WARNING]
> Don’t mix up **je parlerai** (future, “ray”) and **je parlerais** (conditional, “reh”). In careful speech they sound slightly different; in writing, the -s matters.

## Use 1: politeness

The conditional softens requests and wishes — it’s the polite way to ask for things:

- Je voudrais un café, s’il vous plaît. | I’d like a coffee, please.
- Pourriez-vous m’aider ? | Could you help me?
- Est-ce que tu aurais un stylo ? | Would you have a pen?
- J’aimerais vous poser une question. | I’d like to ask you a question.
- Ça vous dérangerait de fermer la fenêtre ? | Would you mind closing the window?

## Use 2: advice, wishes and regrets

- **devoir** in the conditional = should: *Tu **devrais** te reposer.*
- **pouvoir** in the conditional = could, might: *On **pourrait** aller au cinéma.*
- **aimer, vouloir, préférer** = would like / would rather: *Je **préférerais** rester ici.*
- **Il vaudrait mieux** = it would be better: *Il vaudrait mieux partir maintenant.*

- À ta place, je dirais la vérité. | If I were you, I’d tell the truth.
- Ce serait génial ! | That would be great!

## Use 3: hypotheses with si

The golden rule of **si** clauses: **si + imparfait → conditional** for imaginary or unlikely situations:

| Type          | si clause      | Result clause      | Example                                          |
| ------------- | -------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Real / likely | si + present   | present / future / imperative | Si tu viens, on ira au cinéma.        |
| Imaginary     | si + imparfait | conditional        | Si j’avais le temps, je lirais plus.              |
| Past regret   | si + plus-que-parfait | past conditional | Si j’avais su, je serais venu.             |

- Si j’étais riche, j’achèterais une maison au bord de la mer. | If I were rich, I’d buy a house by the sea.
- Si tu parlais plus lentement, je comprendrais. | If you spoke more slowly, I’d understand.
- Qu’est-ce que tu ferais si tu gagnais au loto ? | What would you do if you won the lottery?
- Je viendrais si je pouvais. | I’d come if I could.

> [!WARNING]
> **Never put the conditional (or the future) right after si**: ~~Si j’aurais le temps~~ is a famous mistake — even French people make it. It’s always **si j’avais**. The conditional goes in the *other* half of the sentence.

The two halves can come in either order: *Je lirais plus **si** j’avais le temps.*

## Use 4: future in the past

When reporting what someone **said** or **thought** about the future, the future becomes conditional:

- Il dit qu’il viendra. → Il a dit qu’il viendrait. | He says he’ll come. → He said he would come.
- Je pensais que tu serais là. | I thought you would be there.
- Elle m’a promis qu’elle m’appellerait. | She promised me she would call me.

## Use 5: unconfirmed information

Journalists use the conditional for facts that are **reported but not confirmed** — English uses “reportedly, allegedly, is said to”:

- Le président serait malade. | The president is reportedly ill.
- L’accident aurait fait trois victimes. | The accident is said to have left three victims.

## Quick summary

- Future stem + -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient.
- Politeness: je voudrais, pourriez-vous, j’aimerais.
- Advice: tu devrais, on pourrait, il vaudrait mieux.
- si + imparfait → conditional (never conditional after si).
- Future in the past and unconfirmed news.

- hint: vouloir

- en: I would like a tea, please.
- explain: Polite conditional: je voudrais.

- answer: Pourriez

- explain: vous + pourr- + iez.
