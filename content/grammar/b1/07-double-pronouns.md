## When two pronouns meet

Verbs like *donner, dire, envoyer, montrer, prêter, expliquer, offrir* take two objects: something (direct) **to** someone (indirect). When you replace both with pronouns, they go in a **fixed order** before the verb: *Je donne **le livre à Paul*** → *Je **le lui** donne*.

## The order before the verb

| 1          | 2            | 3          | 4  | 5  |
| ---------- | ------------ | ---------- | -- | -- |
| me, te, se, nous, vous | le, la, les | lui, leur | y  | en |

Read it left to right — at most one pronoun from each column:

- Il me donne le livre. → Il me le donne. | He gives it to me.
- Je donne la lettre à Paul. → Je la lui donne. | I give it to him.
- Elle raconte l’histoire aux enfants. → Elle la leur raconte. | She tells it to them.
- Tu nous montres tes photos ? → Tu nous les montres ? | Will you show them to us?
- Il m’a parlé de son projet. → Il m’en a parlé. | He told me about it.
- Il y a des croissants. → Il y en a. | There are some.

> [!TIP]
> A memory aid: **me/te/nous/vous before le/la/les**, but **le/la/les before lui/leur**. So it’s *je **te le** dis* but *je **le lui** dis*. The third persons (le/lui) go in alphabetical order: **l**e before **l**ui… and **y** before **en** (as in *il y en a*).

## With y and en

**y** and **en** always come last, en very last:

- Je vous en prie. | You’re welcome. / Please.
- Il lui en a donné deux. | He gave him/her two (of them).
- Je t’y emmène. | I’ll take you there.
- Ne m’en parle pas ! | Don’t talk to me about it!

## In compound tenses and with infinitives

The whole group sits before the **auxiliary** in compound tenses, and before the **infinitive** with verb + infinitive. Agreement after avoir follows the **direct** pronoun (le, la, les):

- Je te l’ai déjà dit. | I’ve already told you (that).
- La lettre ? Je la lui ai envoyée hier. | The letter? I sent it to him yesterday.
- Je vais le leur expliquer. | I’m going to explain it to them.
- Tu peux me la prêter ? | Can you lend it to me?
- Elle ne nous les a pas montrées. | She didn’t show them to us.

## The positive imperative: a different order

In **positive** commands, the pronouns go **after** the verb, with hyphens, and the **direct pronoun comes first**: verb – **le/la/les** – **moi/toi/lui/nous/vous/leur** – y – en.

| Statement            | Positive imperative | Negative imperative     |
| -------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Tu me le donnes.     | Donne-le-moi !      | Ne me le donne pas !    |
| Tu la lui envoies.   | Envoie-la-lui !     | Ne la lui envoie pas !  |
| Vous nous les montrez. | Montrez-les-nous ! | Ne nous les montrez pas ! |
| Tu m’en donnes.      | Donne-m’en !        | Ne m’en donne pas !     |

**me / te** become **moi / toi** at the end, but **m’ / t’** before en: *Donne-m’en*, *Va-t’en !* (Go away!).

> [!WARNING]
> Negative commands go back to the normal order: ***Ne me le dis pas*** — not ~~Ne le-moi dis pas~~.

## In everyday speech

Spoken French often simplifies: *Je lui dis* (dropping *le* when it’s obvious), or *Donne-moi-le* in very casual speech. Keep the standard order in writing, but don’t be surprised to hear shortcuts.

## Quick summary

- Order: me/te/se/nous/vous → le/la/les → lui/leur → y → en.
- je te le dis; je le lui dis; il y en a; je vous en prie.
- Positive imperative: verb-le-moi, verb-la-lui, donne-m’en.
- Negative imperative: normal order before the verb.
