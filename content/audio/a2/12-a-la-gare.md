## Scene

You’re at a train station in Paris and you want to go to Lyon for the weekend. The ticket machine isn’t working, so you go to the counter. The agent is called Thomas.

## Dialogue

- Thomas: Bonjour, je vous écoute. | Hello, how can I help?
- Alex: Bonjour. Je voudrais un aller-retour pour Lyon, s’il vous plaît. | Hello. I’d like a return ticket to Lyon, please.
- Thomas: Vous partez quand ? | When are you leaving?
- Alex: Aujourd’hui. Le prochain train part à quelle heure ? | Today. What time does the next train leave?
- Thomas: À quatorze heures dix. Et le retour ? | At ten past two. And the return?
- Alex: Dimanche soir. Il faut changer ? | Sunday evening. Do I have to change?
- Thomas: Non, c’est direct. Ça fait quatre-vingts euros. | No, it’s direct. That’s eighty euros.
- Alex: D’accord. Le train part de quel quai ? | OK. Which platform does the train leave from?
- Thomas: Quai sept. Bon voyage ! | Platform seven. Have a good trip!

## Phrases

- un aller-retour · pour Lyon | a return ticket to Lyon | A one-way ticket is just the first half of this word.
- Vous partez · quand ? | When are you leaving?
- aujourd’hui | today
- Le prochain train · part · à quelle heure ? | What time does the next train leave?
- à quatorze heures | at two in the afternoon | Timetables use the twenty-four hour clock: fourteen hundred hours.
- Il faut · changer ? | Do I have to change?
- C’est direct | It’s direct
- Le train part · de quel quai ? | Which platform does the train leave from?
- Bon voyage ! | Have a good trip!

## Practice

- Je voudrais un aller-retour pour Paris. | Ask for a return ticket to Paris.
- Le prochain train pour Lyon part à quelle heure ? | Ask what time the next train to Lyon leaves.
- Je pars aujourd’hui, et je rentre dimanche. | Say you’re leaving today, and coming back on Sunday.
- C’est direct, ou il faut changer ? | Ask if it’s direct, or if you have to change.
- Merci beaucoup, bonne journée ! | Thank the agent warmly, and wish him a nice day.
