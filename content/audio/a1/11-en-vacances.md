## Scene

You’ve just come back from a short trip to the seaside. Your friend Paul wants to know everything. Some verbs, mostly verbs of moving, build their past with “to be” instead of “to have”. You’ll hear them here.

## Dialogue

- Paul: Tu es rentré quand ? | When did you get back?
- Alex: Je suis rentré hier soir. | I got back last night.
- Paul: Tu es allé où ? | Where did you go?
- Alex: Je suis allé à Biarritz, au bord de la mer. | I went to Biarritz, by the sea.
- Paul: Tu es parti avec qui ? | Who did you go with?
- Alex: Avec ma copine. On est partis en train. | With my girlfriend. We went by train.
- Paul: Vous êtes restés combien de temps ? | How long did you stay?
- Alex: Trois jours. On est allés à la plage tous les jours. | Three days. We went to the beach every day.

## Phrases

- Tu es rentré · quand ? | When did you get back?
- Je suis rentré · hier soir | I got back last night | Coming, going, arriving, leaving and staying use “I am” in the past, not “I have”.
- Tu es allé où ? | Where did you go?
- Je suis allé · au bord de la mer | I went to the seaside | A woman writes it with an extra e. It sounds the same.
- Tu es parti · avec qui ? | Who did you go with?
- On est partis · en train | We went by train
- Vous êtes restés · combien de temps ? | How long did you stay?
- trois jours | three days
- tous les jours | every day
- à la plage | to the beach

## Practice

- Je suis allé à Paris en train. | Say you went to Paris by train.
- Nous sommes restés une semaine. | Say you stayed a week, talking about yourself and someone else.
- Tu es rentré quand ? | Ask a friend when they got back.
- Je suis parti avec ma sœur. | Say you went with your sister.
- On est allés à la plage hier. | Say that you and your friends went to the beach yesterday.
