## Scene

You are having a coffee with your neighbour Claire. She shows you a photo of her family on her phone, and then she asks about yours. You’ll learn to say who people are, how old they are and what they do.

## Dialogue

- Claire: Regarde, c’est ma famille. Voici mon mari, Thomas. | Look, this is my family. Here is my husband, Thomas.
- Alex: Il est très grand ! Et ce sont tes enfants ? | He’s very tall! And are those your children?
- Claire: Oui, mon fils a six ans et ma fille a dix ans. | Yes, my son is six and my daughter is ten.
- Alex: Ils sont mignons. | They’re cute.
- Claire: Merci ! Et toi, tu as des frères et sœurs ? | Thank you! And you, do you have brothers and sisters?
- Alex: J’ai une sœur. Elle s’appelle Anna. Elle est infirmière. | I have a sister. Her name is Anna. She’s a nurse.
- Claire: Elle habite où ? | Where does she live?
- Alex: Elle habite à Toronto, avec ses deux chats. | She lives in Toronto, with her two cats.

## Phrases

- Voici · ma famille | Here is my family
- mon mari | my husband
- Ce sont · tes enfants ? | Are those your children? | Use the friendly form of “your” with a friend.
- Mon fils · a six ans | My son is six | French uses the verb “to have” for age: he has six years.
- ma fille | my daughter
- Ils sont mignons | They’re cute
- Tu as · des frères et sœurs ? | Do you have brothers and sisters?
- J’ai une sœur | I have a sister
- Elle s’appelle Anna | Her name is Anna
- Elle est infirmière | She’s a nurse | No word for “a” before a job.
- Elle habite où ? | Where does she live?

## Practice

- Voici mon frère. Il a vingt ans. | Show a photo of your brother and say he is twenty.
- Ma fille s’appelle Emma. | Say your daughter is called Emma.
- Il est professeur. | Say he is a teacher.
- Tu as des enfants ? | Ask a friend if they have children.
- Mon mari habite à Paris. | Say your husband lives in Paris.
