## Scene

You’re in Paris for the first time. You’re a little lost, so you stop a woman in the street. Her name is Claire. You want to know if she speaks English.

## Dialogue

- Alex: Excusez-moi, madame. | Excuse me, madam.
- Claire: Oui ? | Yes?
- Alex: Vous parlez anglais ? | Do you speak English?
- Claire: Non, je ne parle pas anglais. Vous parlez français ? | No, I don’t speak English. Do you speak French?
- Alex: Un peu. Je ne comprends pas bien. | A little. I don’t understand well.
- Claire: Ah, d’accord ! Pas de problème. | Ah, OK! No problem.
- Alex: Merci, madame. Au revoir ! | Thank you, madam. Goodbye!
- Claire: Au revoir, monsieur ! | Goodbye, sir!

## Phrases

- excusez-moi | excuse me | Use it to get a stranger’s attention, or to say sorry when you bump into someone.
- madame | madam | Add it when you speak to a woman you don’t know. It’s polite, not old-fashioned.
- monsieur | sir | And this is the word for a man.
- Vous parlez · anglais ? | Do you speak English? | In everyday French, a question is often just a statement with your voice going up at the end.
- Je ne parle pas · anglais | I don’t speak English | The negative wraps around the verb: one small word before it, one after it.
- Vous parlez · français ? | Do you speak French?
- un peu | a little
- Je ne comprends pas | I don’t understand
- merci | thank you
- au revoir | goodbye

## Practice

- Excusez-moi, monsieur. Vous parlez anglais ? | Stop a man in the street, and ask if he speaks English.
- Je ne comprends pas, madame. | Tell the woman you don’t understand.
- Un peu. Je ne parle pas français. | Say “a little”, and then that you don’t speak French.
- Merci, monsieur. Au revoir ! | Thank the man and say goodbye.
