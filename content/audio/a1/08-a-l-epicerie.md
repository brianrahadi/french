## Scene

It’s Sunday evening and the supermarket is closed. You go to the small corner shop near your flat to buy something for dinner. The owner, Monsieur Haddad, is behind the counter. You’ll learn to ask for “some” of something, and to say there isn’t any left.

## Dialogue

- M. Haddad: Bonsoir ! Vous cherchez quelque chose ? | Good evening! Are you looking for something?
- Alex: Bonsoir. Vous avez du pain ? | Good evening. Do you have any bread?
- M. Haddad: Oui, il reste deux baguettes. | Yes, there are two baguettes left.
- Alex: Une baguette, s’il vous plaît. Et du fromage. | One baguette, please. And some cheese.
- M. Haddad: Du camembert ou du chèvre ? | Camembert or goat’s cheese?
- Alex: Du camembert. Et je voudrais aussi de l’eau et des tomates. | Camembert. And I’d also like some water and some tomatoes.
- M. Haddad: Désolé, je n’ai plus de tomates. | Sorry, I don’t have any tomatoes left.
- Alex: Pas de problème. Ça fait combien ? | No problem. How much is that?
- M. Haddad: Neuf euros vingt. | Nine euros twenty.

## Phrases

- Vous avez · du pain ? | Do you have any bread? | French needs a little word for “some” before things you can’t count.
- du fromage | some cheese
- de l’eau | some water
- des tomates | some tomatoes
- Je voudrais aussi · de l’eau | I’d also like some water
- Il reste · deux baguettes | There are two baguettes left
- Je n’ai plus · de tomates | I don’t have any tomatoes left | After “not any more”, the little word for “some” becomes just “of”.
- Pas de problème | No problem
- Ça fait combien ? | How much is that?
- Bonsoir ! | Good evening!

## Practice

- Vous avez du lait ? | Ask if they have any milk.
- Je voudrais du fromage et des pommes. | Say you’d like some cheese and some apples.
- Je n’ai plus de pain. | Say you don’t have any bread left.
- Il reste de l’eau ? | Ask if there is any water left.
- Une baguette, s’il vous plaît. Ça fait combien ? | Ask for one baguette, then ask how much it is.
