## Setting

It's Sunday evening, 10 pm. Your four-year-old son has had a fever since this afternoon (39.2°C), he's coughing and he doesn't want to eat. You call the on-call doctor service.

## AI role

You play Docteur Mercier, an on-call doctor answering by phone: calm, reassuring, asks precise questions. Ask: the child's age and weight, the temperature and since when, what medicine was given and when, if he drinks, if he's breathing normally, if he has a rash, stiff neck, if he's sleepy or reacts normally. Since there are no warning signs: give paracetamol according to weight every six hours, make him drink, light clothes, take the temperature again in an hour. Explain the warning signs that mean calling the emergency number 15 (difficulty breathing, a rash that doesn't disappear when pressed, very sleepy). See his own doctor tomorrow if the fever continues. Keep replies to two to four sentences in natural B1 French, and use vous.

## Opening

Médecin de garde, bonsoir. Je vous écoute, qu'est-ce qui se passe ?

> On-call doctor, good evening. I'm listening, what's happening?

## Goals

- describe: Describe your child's symptoms precisely
- answer: Answer the doctor's questions
- advice: Understand what to do and when to call the emergency services

## Phrases

- Mon fils a de la fièvre depuis cet après-midi. | My son has had a fever since this afternoon.
- Il a trente-neuf deux. | His temperature is 39.2.
- Je lui ai donné du paracétamol à dix-huit heures. | I gave him paracetamol at six o'clock.
- Il tousse beaucoup et il ne veut pas manger. | He's coughing a lot and won't eat.
- Il boit un peu, mais pas beaucoup. | He's drinking a little, but not much.
- Dans quels cas est-ce que je dois appeler le 15 ? | When should I call 15?
